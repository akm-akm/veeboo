The problem

Each rep ran four to six customer calls a day. Notes lived in notebooks, in personal documents, or in the rep's head.

The sales manager could not trust the pipeline report, because half the deals had no recent notes and no next step.

Follow-up emails went out the next day at best. Customers who had been keen on the call had cooled by the time the summary arrived.

What we built

Calls are recorded with the customer's consent, transcribed, and turned into a short structured note: what was discussed, objections, budget, who decides, and the agreed next step.

The note is filed against the right deal in the CRM and the deal fields are updated. A follow-up email is drafted in the rep's own style and left in their drafts folder. Nothing is sent to a customer without the rep reading it first.

The automations

Call notes
A one-page summary of each call, in the same five headings every time.
CRM update
Stage, budget, decision maker and next step filled in on the deal record.
Follow-up draft
An email in the rep's tone, with the agreed actions and dates, waiting in drafts.
Manager digest
A Friday summary of every deal that moved, stalled or has no next step.

How it ran

The same four phases as every project.

  1. Audit

    Sat in on a day of calls with two reps and compared what was said with what reached the CRM.

    1 week

  2. Pilot

    Two reps, notes only. They marked each note as right, close or wrong, and the format was tuned.

    2 weeks

  3. Build

    CRM field updates, follow-up drafts for all seven reps, and the Friday digest for the manager.

    4 weeks

  4. Retainer

    Note quality checked on a sample of calls, new products added to the vocabulary, CRM changes tracked.

    Monthly
