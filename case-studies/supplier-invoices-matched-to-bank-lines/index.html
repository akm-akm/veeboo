The problem

The firm looked after the books of about 45 small businesses. Supplier invoices arrived as email attachments, phone photos and the occasional scan.

Each one was typed into the accounting software, then matched by eye to a line on the bank statement. It was careful work, and nearly all of it was routine.

The partners wanted month-end done faster without hiring, and they wanted a person to check anything unusual rather than everything.

What we built

The audit showed that four in five invoices were plain: one supplier, one amount, one matching bank line. Those are now read, coded and matched without anyone touching them.

Anything else goes to an exceptions queue with the reason spelled out: amount differs, possible duplicate, supplier not recognised, or VAT number missing. A bookkeeper decides, and the rule is updated if the same case keeps appearing.

Every automatic match keeps a link to the original document, so a partner can check any entry in two clicks.

The automations

Invoice capture
Attachments and photos sent to one inbox are read and turned into supplier, date, amount and VAT.
Bank line matching
Each invoice is matched to its payment by amount, date and supplier reference.
Exceptions queue
Anything that does not match cleanly is listed with the reason and a suggested fix.
Missing document chaser
Clients get a weekly message listing payments that still have no invoice.
