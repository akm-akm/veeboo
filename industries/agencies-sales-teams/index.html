Where the time goes today

Selling is the job. This is the part that crowds it out.

  • Notes get written at 6pm, or never

    After five calls in a day, the details of the first one are gone. What reaches the CRM is one line.

  • The CRM does not match reality

    Stages, amounts and close dates are updated the night before the pipeline review, so the forecast is a guess.

  • Follow-ups go out late

    The recap email waits behind the next call, and the prospect's interest cools while it does.

  • Handovers lose the detail

    When a deal moves from sales to delivery, or from one rep to another, what the client actually said stays in someone's head.

What we set up

Everything lands where your team already works: the deal record, the task list and the drafts folder.

  • Call notes

    What starts it
    A recorded video or phone call ends.
    What it does
    Writes structured notes in your format: needs, budget, decision makers, objections, competitors mentioned and next steps.
    What a person still does
    The rep skims the notes and corrects anything the recording got wrong.

  • CRM update

    What starts it
    The notes are ready.
    What it does
    Finds the right contact and deal, updates stage, amount and close date, fills the fields you care about, and creates tasks with owners and due dates.
    What a person still does
    For changes you mark as sensitive, such as amount or stage, the rep approves with one click before it saves.

  • Follow-up draft

    What starts it
    The CRM update is done.
    What it does
    Drafts the recap email in the rep's own style, with the agreed next steps and any promised documents listed.
    What a person still does
    The rep edits and sends. Nothing goes to a client without a person pressing send.

  • CRM hygiene sweep

    What starts it
    Every Monday morning, or a schedule you choose.
    What it does
    Lists deals with no next step, close dates in the past, missing amounts and contacts with no owner, and sends each rep their own short list.
    What a person still does
    Reps fix their list. The sales lead sees what is still open.

  • Sales-to-delivery handover

    What starts it
    A deal is marked won.
    What it does
    Builds a brief from every call and email on the deal: what was sold, what was promised, who is who, and the risks raised.
    What a person still does
    The account lead reads it before the kickoff call.

What it will and will not do

  • Recording needs consent

    Call recording rules differ by country and state. The recorder announces itself, and you decide which calls are recorded and how participants are told.

  • A person sends every email

    Follow-ups are drafts. Nothing goes to a client or prospect until a rep has read it and pressed send.

  • Sensitive fields need approval

    You choose which CRM changes save automatically and which wait for a click, so the forecast is never rewritten without the rep seeing it.

  • Your data stays in your accounts

    Recordings, transcripts and notes live in your own CRM and storage, and how long they are kept is your setting. Confidentiality terms you have signed with clients are checked before any call is processed.

  • You own the accounts

    The CRM, the recorder and the workflow accounts are in your company's name. If we stop working together, everything keeps running.

Questions we get asked

Are the notes accurate enough to trust?

They are good on what was said and agreed, and weaker on names, numbers and strong accents, which is why the rep reviews before anything is sent. In the pilot we compare the notes against a sample of calls with you, and tune the format until your team would rather edit them than write their own.

Does this need a bot in every meeting?

Not always. If your meeting tool already records, the automation can work from that recording. Where a notetaker joins the call, it announces itself. You choose which meetings are covered.

We tried a notetaker app and nobody used it. Why is this different?

A summary that sits in a separate app is one more place to look. This puts the result where the work already happens: the deal record, the task list and the drafts folder. Reps do less, not one more thing.

Can it follow our sales method?

Yes. If you qualify with a set of questions, such as budget, authority, need and timing, the notes and CRM fields follow that structure, and the hygiene sweep flags deals where an answer is missing.

What about calls with confidential client details?

You can exclude clients, meeting types or individual calls. Recordings and notes stay in your own accounts, and we agree retention and access in writing before the build.
