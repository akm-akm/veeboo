Where the time goes today

A front desk has one pair of hands. These are the jobs that lose out.

  • Calls go unanswered at the busiest times

    The front desk is checking a patient in, taking a payment and answering the phone at once. A caller who gets voicemail may simply book somewhere else.

  • Reminders are done by hand, or not at all

    Someone works through tomorrow's list by phone when there is a quiet half hour. On a busy day there is none, and the no-shows follow.

  • Recalls slip

    Patients due a check-up or hygiene appointment sit in a report nobody has time to work through.

  • Cancellations leave gaps

    A late cancellation leaves an empty chair, even though there are patients who wanted an earlier slot.

What we set up

You can start with reminders and recalls only, and add booking once you trust it.

  • WhatsApp and phone booking

    What starts it
    A patient messages or calls, at any hour.
    What it does
    Answers, finds a slot in your practice management system for the right appointment type and clinician, books it and confirms in writing.
    What a person still does
    The front desk handles anything unusual. Clinical questions and complaints go to them with the conversation attached.

  • Appointment reminders

    What starts it
    A set time before each appointment, such as two days and two hours.
    What it does
    Sends a reminder with a one-tap confirm or reschedule. Unconfirmed appointments are flagged for a call.
    What a person still does
    Staff call only the patients who did not confirm.

  • Patient recall

    What starts it
    A patient becomes due for a check-up, hygiene appointment or review.
    What it does
    Sends a recall message with available slots, follows up on a schedule you set, and books the reply.
    What a person still does
    You set who is recalled and when. The assistant never changes a recall interval a clinician has set.

  • Cancellation and waiting-list fill

    What starts it
    An appointment is cancelled or moved.
    What it does
    Offers the freed slot to patients on the waiting list who wanted that kind of appointment. The first to reply gets it.
    What a person still does
    The front desk approves the list rules, such as who is offered short-notice slots.

  • Urgent case handover

    What starts it
    A message or call mentions pain, swelling, bleeding, an injury or anything else on your urgent list.
    What it does
    Stops the booking conversation, gives your emergency instructions, and alerts the member of staff on duty straight away.
    What a person still does
    A clinician or trained member of staff decides what happens next. The assistant never assesses symptoms.

What it will and will not do

  • No medical advice

    The assistant books, reminds and answers practical questions such as opening hours, parking and what to bring. It does not assess symptoms, suggest treatment or comment on medication.

  • Urgent cases go to a person

    You give us the words and situations that count as urgent. When one comes up, the assistant stops, gives your emergency instructions and alerts your team.

  • Patient data is agreed per project

    Which data the assistant can see, where it is processed and how long it is kept are written down before anything is built. If your practice falls under HIPAA or UK GDPR, the tools, agreements and hosting are chosen with you to fit, and you confirm them with your own compliance adviser.

  • It says it is an assistant

    Patients are told they are talking to an automated assistant and can ask for a member of staff at any point.

  • You own the accounts

    The phone number, WhatsApp account and workflow accounts belong to the practice.

Questions we get asked

Is it safe to let an AI talk to patients?

It is limited to booking, reminders and practical questions, and it is told what it must not do. Anything clinical, urgent or upset goes to your team with the conversation attached. You read and approve every script before it goes live.

What about HIPAA or UK GDPR?

It depends on your practice and the tools involved, so we agree it in writing per project: what data is used, where it is processed and which agreements are needed. We will tell you plainly if a tool you want cannot be used the way your rules require.

Does it work with our practice management system?

If your system offers an API or an approved integration, the assistant can read free slots and write bookings directly. If it does not, there are slower routes, such as a shared booking calendar that staff confirm. We check this first, in the audit.

Can we start small?

Yes. Reminders and recalls alone are a sensible first step, because they touch no clinical decisions. Booking by WhatsApp or phone can be added later. The assistant is always clear about what it is, and a person is one message away.

Can it answer the phone, or only WhatsApp?

Both. The voice line can answer every call, or only the ones your front desk cannot pick up within a few rings. Each call is summarised in writing so staff can see what was booked.
