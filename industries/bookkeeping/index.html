Where the time goes today

The judgement is the valuable part of the job. The matching is what eats the week.

  • Bank feeds that still need a person on every line

    Bank rules catch the obvious. The rest is someone clicking through lines, one client at a time.

  • Chasing clients for receipts and invoices

    The same emails, to the same clients, every month, and the close waits for the replies.

  • Month-end piles up

    Coding and matching are left until the last week, so the close runs late and the team works evenings to get it out.

  • Senior time spent on junior work

    The people who should be reviewing and advising are keying supplier bills.

What we set up

It works alongside the bank rules you already have, and every proposal shows why it was made.

  • Bank line matching

    What starts it
    New lines arrive on a client's bank feed.
    What it does
    Matches each line to an open invoice, bill or receipt by amount, date, reference and payee, and proposes the entry with a confidence score and its reason.
    What a person still does
    A bookkeeper approves proposed matches in batches and works the exceptions.

  • Bill and receipt capture

    What starts it
    A client emails, uploads or photographs a document.
    What it does
    Reads the supplier, date, amounts and tax, suggests the account code from that client's history, and attaches the document to the entry.
    What a person still does
    Anything new, unclear or over a limit you set waits for review.

  • Missing-document chaser

    What starts it
    A bank line has no document after a number of days you choose.
    What it does
    Sends the client one tidy list of what is missing, with a link to upload, and reminds them on your schedule.
    What a person still does
    The client manager steps in for clients who do not respond.

  • Exceptions queue

    What starts it
    A line cannot be matched or breaks a rule, such as a duplicate, a part payment or an unknown payee.
    What it does
    Puts it in one queue per client with the likely explanation and the nearest candidates.
    What a person still does
    A bookkeeper decides. Their decision can become a rule for next time, if they approve it.

  • Month-end close checklist

    What starts it
    The last working day of the month, or your own close calendar.
    What it does
    Checks each client for unreconciled lines, open exceptions, missing documents and unusual balances, and sends the status to the client manager.
    What a person still does
    The reviewer signs off the close.

What it will and will not do

  • A person approves exceptions

    The automation proposes. A bookkeeper decides anything that is not a clean match, and can see why each match was suggested.

  • Nothing posts unreviewed unless you choose

    By default every entry waits for approval. If you decide that certain clean matches may post automatically for a client, that is your written rule, with limits, and it can be switched off.

  • Every entry has a trail

    Each proposed and approved entry records the source document, the reason for the match and who approved it.

  • Client data stays in your systems

    The work runs through your accounting software's own access controls. Which data leaves it, where it is processed and for how long are agreed in writing first.

  • Not tax or accounting advice

    The automation does matching and chasing. Treatment, judgement and sign-off stay with your qualified staff.

Questions we get asked

How is this different from the bank rules in our accounting software?

Bank rules match on fixed text. This reads the document as well as the line, so it can handle references that change, part payments, batched payouts and suppliers it has not seen in that exact form. It works alongside your existing rules and leaves them in place.

Can it post entries by itself?

Only if you choose that, client by client, for the kinds of match you define. The default is that everything waits for a bookkeeper to approve.

What happens when it gets one wrong?

Proposed matches show their reason, so a wrong one is quick to spot and reject. Rejections are logged and used to adjust the rules. In the pilot we measure how many proposals your team accepts unchanged before anything is widened.

How do you get access to client ledgers?

Through a user or app connection that you create and control in your accounting software, limited to the clients in scope. You can remove it at any time. We do not hold client bank logins.

Does it handle multi-currency and sales tax or VAT?

It reads currency and tax amounts from the documents and proposes the tax code from each client's history. Unusual treatments go to the exceptions queue for a person.
